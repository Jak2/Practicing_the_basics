## The window (open until disbursement — days, not months)

The loan isn't disbursed and the EMI isn't fixed, so the *shape* of the funding can still change even though the decision can't:

1. **Itemize and challenge the ₹7L before signing.** A lean wash is ₹1.5–3L of equipment + deposit. If the ₹7L estimate came from an equipment vendor or franchise salesman, it's their profit margin, not the market's requirement. Borrowing less = smaller EMI forever.
2. **Take PMEGP to the bank BEFORE disbursement.** Owner has clean credit + the wash is a new unit + no prior subsidy = textbook PMEGP case *in the owner's own name*. Project ₹10L → owner contributes ~₹1L, bank funds the rest, 15–25% margin-money subsidy (15% urban general / 25% rural general — Ongole city vs panchayat-area site matters). If PMEGP funds the wash, the mortgage shrinks to: killing expensive old debt + working-capital buffer only. One DIC Ongole visit + Udyam registration starts it. Risk: PMEGP sanction takes 2–6 months — decide whether the wash timeline can absorb that.
3. **The buffer rule (non-negotiable given zero family funds):** whatever is finally borrowed, **6 months of ALL obligations (old EMIs + new EMI + wash rent/wages) sits untouched in an FD** before a single rupee goes to construction. A business opened with zero buffer dies at its first slow month — and this family has no second chance behind this money.
4. **Lease trap:** building civil works on leased land = gifting value to the landlord. Minimum 5-year *registered* lease with renewal option and rent-escalation cap, or find a site needing no civil work.

**The 29-year question:** the owner's real asset is three decades of travels relationships — drivers, customers, repeat clients. ## Track 1 — Protect the unspent mortgage money

- Every rupee not yet spent or contractually committed goes to **retiring the highest-interest debt** (any private/hand loan at 24–36% dies first). Cheap secured money killing expensive money is a permanent EMI cut.

## Track 2 — Build a car wash that survives in Ongole

**Reality check (Jaya's correction, he's right): truck/lorry crews wash their own vehicles** — the driver-cum-cleaner does it at water points. Buses mostly likewise. So there is no commercial-fleet anchor. The paying market in a tier-3 town is:

1. **Two-wheelers** — the volume. Bikes outnumber cars ~10:1 in tier-3; ₹50–80/wash, fast turnaround.
2. **Private car regulars via monthly pass** — ₹500–600/month for 4 washes. Subscription = predictable cash (same logic as monthly duty for the cabs). Target: doctors, lawyers, business owners, government staff.
3. **Used-car dealers and bike showrooms** — pre-delivery and reconditioning washes. The only real B2B left; small but steady.
4. **Detailing/polishing is the margin product** — interior cleaning, polishing (₹1,500–3,000/job), seat covers, headlight restoration — aimed at the town's growing SUV owners (Creta/Scorpio/Thar class). The ₹150–250 water wash is a commodity hook; detailing is the profit.
5. **Pickup-and-drop wash — the differentiator nobody in Ongole offers:** travels drivers are idle between duties; they collect the customer's car, wash, return it. Zero extra capex, uses existing staff, justifies a premium, and every pickup is a cab-service advertisement.

**Rules before opening:**
1. **Pre-sell before finishing construction:** 20–30 monthly passes and 1–2 dealer arrangements. If the town won't pre-commit even at a discount, that is the market answering — stop spending.
2. **Lean spec:** manual + pressure washers (₹1.5–3L total), borewell water (municipal water will kill margins), 2 workers. Location: where the private cars and bikes are — near hospital/court/business cluster — not highway frontage. No automatic systems (₹8–10L+ metro-throughput machines).
3. **Synergy:** the wash doubles as the travels booking office/taxi adda. Drivers wash the fleet between duties. Every wash customer hears about the cab service; every cab customer hears about the wash.

**The one number on the wall:** monthly obligation ÷ (30 × average ticket) = vehicles/day needed to break even.
Example: rent ₹15k + labor ₹25k + utilities/consumables ₹12k + mortgage EMI ₹40k = ₹92k/month → at a bike-heavy blended ticket of ~₹150 that's **~20 vehicles/day, every day, just to break even** (at ₹250 blended, ~12/day). ### Market reality check (crosschecked 2026-07-03)

- Ongole ≈ **3.05 lakh people (2026 est.)**, up from 2.05 lakh in 2011 — genuinely growing district HQ that pulls surrounding-village spending (hospitals, courts, colleges).
- Rough vehicle base at tier-3 penetration (~30 cars/1,000 people): **~9,000 cars** in city + catchment; **~75–90k two-wheelers**. Of car owners, realistically only 10–15% ever pay a shop to wash — a serviceable pool of maybe **1,000–1,500 cars, of which 300–600 become regulars**, split among existing players.
- Competition exists but is thin: a handful of washes on Kurnool Road / 60 Feet Road, plus showroom service stations that wash with servicing. No strong detailing brand.
- **Honest ceiling:** a well-run lean wash in this market matures at roughly ₹40–80k/month net. That is a real business worth having — and still nowhere near ₹2L/month. The wash is a cash-flow tributary; Track 3 + debt restructuring remain the river.
- Existing tier-3 washes typically do 8–15 vehicles/day — so a 20/day break-even target is genuinely demanding. Obligations must be kept low (cheap rent, 2 workers, no franchise fees) or the model dies.
- **Best ground-truth test, costs one afternoon:** stand near the existing washes and count vehicles/hour, note their prices and queue times. That one field visit beats every estimate in this file.

**System verdict for Ongole:** manual pressure-wash + detailing corner + pickup/drop via idle travels drivers. **No franchise** (5K Car Care / Ola Car Wash etc. charge ₹3–10L fees + royalty — they profit from franchisees, not with them; unaffordable in a debt crisis). **No automatic machines.**

## Funding Track B — Private investor on profit share (no equity)

The model Jaya described is legitimate and common — but structure decides whether it ends in growth or in a police complaint. Rules:

1. **Revenue share, not profit share.** Profit in a cash business is unverifiable → guaranteed disputes. A % of *gross collections* is auditable if all payments run through UPI/QR + one bank account. Also legal: under the Indian Partnership Act, sharing *profits* is prima facie evidence of partnership — an undocumented profit-share investor can be deemed a partner, with liability mess for everyone.
2. **Cap and term.** E.g., investor puts ₹5L, receives 8–10% of monthly gross until they've been paid 1.7–2× their money or 4 years, whichever first. Capped, finite, verifiable. (This is informal revenue-based financing; formal RBF firms like GetVantage/Klub only serve online businesses — tier-3 offline means known local investors: relatives, granite-money families, NRI kin.)
3. **Ring-fence it.** Investor money enters a separate entity/account for the wash (even a partnership firm or a proprietorship in the operating family member's name) — never the debt-laden proprietorship. Creditors of the old debt must not be able to touch it, and investor money must NEVER pay old EMIs — that's how "investment" becomes a cheating case later.
4. **Paper it.** CA/lawyer-drafted agreement: amount, share %, cap, term, monthly statement obligation, exit clause, dispute resolution. Costs ₹5–10k. Non-negotiable.
5. **Disclose.** The investor hears the true debt situation before signing. Only known persons, no public solicitation (BUDS Act).

## Track 3 — Fix the travels business (biggest lever, zero capex)

1. **Monthly-duty contracts for both cars.** Compact sedans are exactly the field-staff segment: banks, insurance, granite company staff movement, hospitals, government tenders. One contract (₹45–70k/month with driver) converts a car from lottery income to salary income. Two contracts = whole fleet predictable.
2. **Become the dispatcher.** Attach other owner-drivers' cars (including an Innova for premium/airport runs — granite buyers visiting from abroad) under the ASJ name for 10–15% commission. One WhatsApp booking number + rate card + a filled-out Google Business profile (photos, reviews, click-to-WhatsApp).
3. **Register as local vendor** with aggregators already taking Ongole bookings and subcontracting: Savaari, CabBazar, WTicabs. Costs nothing, fills empty days.
4. **Rate card routes:** Tirupati packages, Vijayawada/Guntur/airport, hospital trips, wedding-season blocks.
5. **Sell the Aspire if it runs under ~15 duty days/month.** Ford left India in 2021 — parts/service shrinking, resale falls every year; it's worth more today than it will ever be. Proceeds go against the highest-interest debt. Keep the Xcent (Hyundai network + taxi resale demand).
6. **Check CNG:** if the cars are petrol and Ongole has a CNG station (check AG&P Pratham locator / ask any auto driver), a ₹50–60k retrofit cuts fuel cost ~⅓. Fuel is half of gross. Only after the debt picture is on paper.
7. **Debt restructuring at the bank:** if loans are bank/NBFC and still "standard," formally request MSME restructuring — tenure extension, EMI reduction, moratorium (Udyam registration is free and unlocks the framework). Sit with the branch manager + a local CA. If truly unserviceable even after restructuring, a negotiated settlement + orderly downsizing beats slow personal ruin — keep it on the table honestly.
