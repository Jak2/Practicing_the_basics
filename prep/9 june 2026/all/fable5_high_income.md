The uncomfortable math
A millionaire (₹8–9 crore net worth if we mean USD, ₹1 crore if we mean the Indian usage) is almost never made by one viral thing. It's made by income − expenses, compounded over years, or by equity in something that grows. Everyone selling you a "system" or "funnel" course got rich selling the course, not running the funnel.

The three realistic paths, ranked by probability for you specifically
1. High income from skills (highest probability). You're an engineer with AI/LLM project experience in the hottest hiring market of the decade. A strong AI engineering role in India pays ₹30–80L, remote US-based work pays $100–200k. That's "lakhs per month" — literally the goal you stated — with near-100% probability if you execute the job search well. This is boring, which is why people skip it, and it's also the fastest route. Your career-ops pipeline is already pointed at this.

2. Income + a cash-flow side business (medium probability). Keep the job, run one thing on the side that sells to businesses, not consumers — businesses pay 10–100x what consumers do. Your ContentPilot-as-a-service idea fits. Two or three ₹25–50k retainers on top of a salary changes your savings rate dramatically. The trap to avoid: hopping between five ideas. One offer, sold repeatedly, improved monthly.

3. Equity/startup (low probability, highest ceiling). Building a product that scales is the only path to crores in a few years, but the base rate is brutal — most make nothing for 1–2 years. This only makes sense after path 1 gives you savings runway, or if a side project from path 2 shows genuine pull (people chasing you to buy).

The principles that actually matter
Sell to people with money. B2B > B2C. "AI influencer images" is B2C attention farming; "content automation for agencies" is B2B. Same skills, 10x the price.
Distribution beats product. You're a builder, so you'll over-invest in building and under-invest in selling. Whatever you do, spend at least half your time on getting it in front of buyers — posting, DMing, pitching. The best system in your workspace earns ₹0 if nobody sees it.
Speed of money ∝ closeness to the transaction. Fastest: services (paid this month). Medium: products (paid in months). Slowest: audience/content (paid in year 2). Chasing "viral" is choosing the slowest path while feeling like the fastest.
Don't confuse in-demand with easy to win. Funnels are in demand and every marketer on Earth sells them. The edge isn't the funnel — it's a specific niche where you have proof. Your proof is: "I built an autonomous AI content system. Here it is running."
What I'd tell you as a friend
Run paths 1 and 2 simultaneously for the next 6 months: land the best job you can (that alone can hit your lakhs/month goal), and sell ContentPilot as a service to a handful of clients. If the side business pulls hard, you'll know — and then you consider path 3 from a position of strength, not desperation. Get-rich-in-one-month thinking is what keeps people poor for years; get-rich-in-three-years thinking actually works.
